Campaign 01 · Co-Creation

Design the Next Golden Goose.

"This is your blank canvas. What would you create?"

The idea

What if the next Golden Goose sneaker were designed by its own community? Design the Next Golden Goose is a social Co-Creation campaign that invites people to reinterpret a completely white sneaker and submit their own design.

The blank sneaker becomes a canvas for individual expression: colours, illustrations, type, materials and personal references. At the end, with the community's support, one winning concept is selected and developed with Golden Goose's creative team, with the ambition of turning it into a real product. Its author is publicly credited as the original creative behind it.

The audience stops watching and becomes part of the project.

The insight

"Which one do you prefer, A or B?" "What doesn't exist yet?"

Social audiences increasingly expect to take part in the brands they follow. Polls, customisation content and creative challenges involve people more than one-way product communication.

Golden Goose has a unique opportunity, because customisation is already in its DNA. Instead of asking which existing product people prefer, the campaign gives them a bigger role: imagining something that doesn't exist yet. And the chance of seeing it made for real is a tangible reason to take part, follow along and come back.

  1. Phase 1

    The blank canvas

    Golden Goose launches with an image or short film: a completely white sneaker on a minimal background. The creative is deliberately simple, leaving the product open to interpretation.

    The brand explains the rules, submission format, timeline and selection criteria, making it clear the winning concept may become a physical product, subject to creative approval and production feasibility.

  2. Phase 2

    The design challenge

    Anyone can propose their version, in three ways: a digital design, a hand-drawn concept photographed or scanned, or a real physical customisation, photographed or filmed.

    You don't need to be a designer: originality and the strength of the idea matter more than technique. Every entry is published with the campaign hashtag and format, starting from an official blank-sneaker template so entries can be compared fairly.

    #DesignTheNextGG
  3. Phase 3

    The community shortlist

    When submissions close, the brand selects a shortlist. Each concept gets the same presentation across carousels, Stories and short videos: the design, the creator's name and a short note on the inspiration.

    Then the community votes, in Stories or on a dedicated page. This is the second wave: finalists share their designs and get friends and followers to vote.

  4. Phase 4

    The final selection

    The winner is chosen through a transparent process combining community voting and a Golden Goose creative jury. For example: 70% community vote, 30% jury, judging originality, brand relevance and production feasibility.

    Weights and criteria are defined and announced before launch. The community gets a real voice, and the chosen design still meets the brand's standards.

  5. Phase 5

    From concept to product

    The winner is revealed through a dedicated campaign. Golden Goose documents the development: how the original submission is reviewed and turned into a production-ready design with the creative team.

    The winner is credited as the concept's originator, with credit, compensation, rights and involvement agreed in advance. If approved, the finished sneaker launches across the brand's channels and the winner's own. Not just a contest: a complete story, from a blank canvas to a design created by someone in the community.

Creators and community

The launch brings in design-oriented creators, sneaker enthusiasts, illustrators and fashion personalities. They don't all make the same promo video: each one interprets the blank sneaker through their own creative process. That shows different ways to take part, and makes clear the challenge isn't only for professionals.

Golden Goose's official channels stay the central destination for submissions, finalists, voting and the final reveal.

  • ✎The illustratordevelops a hand-drawn concept
  • ✂The customiserexplores materials and textures on the real shoe
  • ★The fashion creatorbuilds the design around a personal story or cultural reference

From participation to brand value

To take part, people spend time inside Golden Goose's visual identity, its product details and its approach to self-expression. Content can present existing designs and customisation services alongside the challenge, linking personal creativity to real products.

It also reaches people into sneakers, fashion and design who don't follow the brand yet. The strongest outcome isn't the number of entries: it's turning creative participation into lasting interest and product consideration.

Objectives

  • More meaningful engagement through active creative participation.
  • Original, community-created content tied to the brand.
  • Stronger brand affinity through a shared design experience.
  • Introduce Golden Goose to new audiences in fashion, sneakers and creativity.
  • Interest in existing products and customisation services.
  • A story that runs from social participation to a possible physical launch.

Rules first, promises later

Eligibility, deadlines, voting, judging criteria, intellectual property and the prize are all defined before launch.

Participants keep the rights they're entitled to. Any commercial use of the winning design is covered by clear, agreed terms.

It needs originality checks, a feasibility review and room for the creative team to adapt the final design.

The commitment to produce the sneaker is only announced once feasibility, budget and production are approved.

Why it fits Golden Goose

It turns self-expression into an active brand experience. Creative freedom, community voting and the chance of a real product give people a reason to keep coming back. And on social it becomes a story: a shoe starts as a blank canvas, grows through the community's creativity and ends as a design developed with the brand.

Campaign 02 · Storytime

Show Me Your Crimes.

"Officer, I can explain..."

What if your sneakers could testify against you?

GOLDEN GOOSE P.D. · CASE #000

The idea

Show Me Your Crimes is a social-first campaign that turns the worn-in look of sneakers into a storytelling opportunity. Inspired by the visual language of police interrogations and confessions, it invites people to reveal the most outrageous, unexpected or unforgettable experiences behind their favourite shoes.

Golden Goose's distinctive aesthetic becomes the starting point of a playful social experiment: every mark, crease and sign of wear can become evidence of a life well lived. An everyday product turns into a source of humour, personality and participation.

The insight

People attach memories to the things they wear every day. A pair of sneakers can stand for a festival, a spontaneous trip, a memorable night out or a period of change.

On social, personal confessions, relatable humour and storytime formats push people to share their own experiences and tag their friends. Golden Goose can bring these behaviours together in a recognisable format that links its lived-in aesthetic to real stories, moving beyond product-focused content.

Not what your shoes are. What they've been through with you.

The mechanic: an interrogation in three acts

ACT 1 · THE OPENING HOOK

The campaign launches with a series of short videos made with selected creators. Each one follows the same interrogation-style structure, and always opens with the same line:

"Officer, I can explain..."

The creator presents their Golden Goose sneakers as if they were evidence in a case, then reveals the story behind them. It should feel personal, spontaneous and funny, not like a scripted ad.

  • Exhibit ASurvived three music festivals in one weekend.
  • Exhibit BCrossed four countries without ever going home.
  • Exhibit CWorn on an impulsive 2 a.m. adventure.
  • Exhibit DIn every photo of a five-year relationship.
ACT 2 · THE COMMUNITY CHALLENGE

After the creator launch, it's the audience's turn: people post a TikTok or Reel that opens with the campaign line and uses the dedicated hashtag, show their shoes and confess the story behind them.

They can also nominate friends whose sneakers have an equally questionable past. The challenge is easy to reproduce, recognisable in the first few seconds and flexible enough to spark endless interpretations.

#ShowMeYourCrimes
ACT 3 · THE GOLDEN GOOSE POLICE DEPARTMENT

Golden Goose's official channels become the hub of the campaign through a recurring editorial series. The brand picks the most entertaining submissions and presents each one as a case: case number, photos, the owner's confession and a final verdict.

The visual identity stays the same in every episode, so the series builds recognition over time. The community can nominate new cases, vote for the funniest confession and suggest future episode themes.

Creators: same line, different stories

Not only fashion influencers: the campaign needs distinct personalities and lifestyles, people whose content already features memorable experiences. Each creator launches the challenge with their own story, keeping the same opening line while adapting the narrative to their audience.

The result is a network of connected videos: one consistent campaign identity, many storytelling styles. The best creator content can then be amplified through paid social, with the right usage rights.

  • Travel creators
  • Musicians

The goal isn't to turn every video into a sales message. It's to create a natural path from entertainment to product consideration.

  • User-generated content and organic participation.
  • A stronger link between Golden Goose and self-expression.
  • A recurring social format that lives on after launch.
  • Qualified traffic to product pages and retail experiences.

Campaign 03 · Creator-led

The Clean Girl Gave Up.

What happens when the perfect aesthetic becomes exhausting?

The idea

The Clean Girl Gave Up is a creator-led social campaign that plays with the tension between perfectly curated online identities and the unpredictability of real life.

It begins inside the familiar clean girl world: neutral outfits, immaculate spaces, perfect hair, organised routines and seemingly effortless perfection. Then the story changes: the protagonist looks straight into the camera, drops the routine and puts on her Golden Goose sneakers. The music shifts, the editing speeds up, and spontaneous moments take over: a missed train, an unexpected night out, a concert, a late dinner, a new friendship.

Looking polished isn't wrong. But life doesn't need to be perfectly curated to be worth living.

The insight

Social media constantly shows us highly curated lives and aesthetic trends. They're aspirational, but the repetition opens room for irony, parody and a desire for more spontaneous self-expression.

The clean girl aesthetic is instantly recognisable. Using its rules and then breaking them creates a transformation people understand in seconds. That links Golden Goose to the tension between perfection and individuality, without criticising other brands or people who love minimal style.

POV: You're trying to have your life together.

...

Maybe you don't need to have it all together.

  1. Phase 1

    The perfect life

    Each creator opens with a polished sequence that embraces the clean girl conventions: a neutral bedroom, an organised wardrobe, a prepared outfit, a morning routine edited to look effortless. It must be precise and instantly recognisable. On-screen text sets up the premise.

  2. Phase 2

    The switch

    A small moment interrupts the routine: a look straight into the camera, a change in the music, an unexpected gesture. The creator puts on her Golden Goose sneakers. This transition becomes the campaign's recurring visual signature: the sneakers mark the shift from a controlled aesthetic to a more spontaneous everyday life.

  3. Phase 3

    Real life takes over

    The video speeds into a less predictable sequence: running for a train, going out without a plan, dancing at a concert, eating late, bumping into friends. It should feel energetic and personal, not staged chaos: the point isn't to manufacture imperfection but to celebrate experiences that don't follow a script. Then the closing line.

  4. Phase 4

    The community joins in

    Once the creator videos set the format, the audience is invited to make their own version: recreate the transition, show the gap between their curated online persona and everyday life, or share a moment they chose an experience over perfection. Golden Goose features the best interpretations on its official channels.

Who builds the aesthetic Fashion and beauty creators

Set up the clean girl visual language in the first seconds.

Who breaks it Lifestyle, travel, music, comedy

Reinterpret the concept through their own routines and experiences.

The contrast between creators matters: the campaign doesn't prescribe one alternative aesthetic, it shows individuality takes many forms. Everyone follows the same structure, with their own personality, editing style and humour. A coherent campaign, without identical videos.

The content ecosystem, adapted to each platform

  1. 01Transformation Reelsshort videos built on the switch from perfection to spontaneity, on TikTok and Instagram
  2. 02Behind-the-scenes Storiesinformal clips of what really happened in the unplanned moments
  3. 03Community remixesaudience interpretations, reactions and alternative endings
  4. 04Editorial follow-upsshort pieces on personal style, spontaneity and memorable days

The link to the product

Golden Goose appears naturally in the transformation itself: the sneakers are the visual element that makes it recognisable, not an unrelated product placement. Creator captions and follow-up content identify the models and lead to product pages.

Further content shows how different styles work across outfits and aesthetics. That reaches people who already recognise the brand but haven't yet considered it for themselves.

Objectives

  • More relevance with digitally engaged fashion and lifestyle audiences.
  • A place in conversations about personal style and self-expression.
  • Recognisable, repeatable short-form video formats.
  • Audience participation through relatable transformations.
  • Product discovery among people drawn to the brand's aesthetic.

Why it fits Golden Goose

It uses a familiar social aesthetic as a narrative device, then brings a recognisable Golden Goose into the moment of transformation. It positions the brand around freedom of expression and a less rigid approach to style, and gives creators a simple, adaptable format with strong visual storytelling potential.
